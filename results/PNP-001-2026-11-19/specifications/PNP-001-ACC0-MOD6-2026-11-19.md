# PNP-001 priority direction — positive 1-in-3-SAT against circuits with mod-2 and mod-3 gates (owner decision 2026-11-19)

Binding. Research and verification only; no infrastructure changes. Goal: a provable statement about EVERY circuit of a class (not about a
proof system). Not a P != NP claim; the target ceiling is NP not in ACC0 (open; best known NQP not in ACC0, Williams / Murray-Williams).

## Why this direction
GF(3)-blind cores are exactly the unsatisfiable formulas where GF(3) linear reasoning (consistent) and GF(2) linear reasoning (x = all-ones
always solves the XOR system) both fail. Combining moduli 2 and 3 is where circuit lower bounds stop: AC0[p] (p prime) has lower bounds via
low-degree GF(p) approximation (Razborov-Smolensky); for modulus 6 the method fails.

## Object
SAT_N^{1in3}: input = indicator bits of the triples present on n variables (N = C(n,3)); output 1 iff the positive 1-in-3 formula is
satisfiable. Also restrictions/projections of it (families such as the 3-colouring translation T(G), blind cores, GF(3)-consistent inputs).

## Level 1 (bounded, aim: complete proof)
(a) TRANSFER AUDIT first (it worked for the PC degree): which known circuit lower bounds carry over to SAT_N^{1in3} by projections /
AC0 reductions? E.g. if MOD_q or MAJORITY is a projection of SAT_N^{1in3} (gadgets with auxiliary variables), AC0[p] lower bounds (p != q)
follow from Razborov-Smolensky; state exactly which functions embed and how.
(b) Depth-2 classes SYM o AND_k and MOD_6 o AND_k: Hastad-Goldmann - size s gives a (k+1)-party NOF protocol of cost O(k log s). Is a NOF-hard
function (generalised inner product, Babai-Nisan-Szegedy, k < log n) a projection of SAT_N^{1in3} with the right partition? Then size
lower bounds for these classes follow. Keep fan-in, party count and partition explicit.

## Level 2 (approximating polynomials over Z_6)
Degree of pairs (GF(2) polynomial, GF(3) polynomial) - via CRT a Z_6 polynomial - that represent / weakly represent / approximate
SAT_N^{1in3} or its restrictions; known reference points: Barrington-Beigel-Rudich (symmetric Z_6 representations of OR of degree
O(sqrt n)), Razborov-Smolensky, Green-type weak-degree results. Test objects: blind cores (complete tables n <= 10), satisfiable controls,
the T(G) family. Question: does the GF(2)-and-GF(3) failure of linear reasoning on blind cores force high Z_6 approximation degree?

## Rules
State every result at level 1 (finite) / 2 (a circuit or proof class) / 3 (all algorithms). Barrier check from the start: natural proofs
(state constructivity and largeness; for ACC0 the barrier applies only if ACC0 computes pseudorandom functions), relativisation,
algebrisation. Existing algorithm-to-lower-bound transfers (Williams) count. Finite computations only where they decide a precise question.

## Level 3 (owner deep research 2026-11-20): algorithmic BACKWARD transfer (Williams-type)
Proved mechanism: a Circuit-SAT algorithm for a circuit class C that beats 2^n by a superpolynomial factor (n = circuit INPUTS) gives
circuit lower bounds against C (Williams: NEXP not in ACC0; Murray-Williams: NQP; Chukhin-Gajulapalli-Golovnev-Mihajlin 2026: PIT-based
win-win NE vs VP0_Z != VNP0_Z).
Binding obligations:
- QUANTIFY THE TSEITIN GAP: a (k-)CNF algorithm in 2^{O(t/sqrt(log t))} on t ~ s auxiliary variables of a size-s circuit is worse than
  2^n brute force for s = poly(n). A claimed transfer must state savings relative to 2^n in the circuit's inputs, with reduction cost
  and variable growth explicit.
- Concrete question: does the GF(3) kernel structure of positive 1-in-3 (GF(3)-linear system + two-value condition; one MOD-3 layer)
  give a SAT/#SAT saving for a class such as AND o MOD3 o AND or ACC0[3]-type circuits WITH n INPUTS? Compare with the known ACC0
  #SAT algorithms (the target would have to exceed what already gives NQP not in ACC0).
- Forward/backward identity F in SAT <=> exists s: L(s) and R(s) is exact; Theorem 30 (w*(F(G)) = 2 tw(G)+1, Lean) gives the optimal
  width for the kernel-basis representation. Mandatory counter-checks for any interface/width argument: EQUALITY (rank 2^m, circuit
  O(m)) and the 3-colouring expander construction F(G).
- Barriers (relativisation, natural proofs, algebrisation) checked against the CONCRETE method, not dismissed or assumed.
Ceiling unchanged: NP not in ACC0 (or a Circuit-SAT transfer statement for a stated class) - not P != NP.

## Level 3 precisions (owner 2026-11-20, binding)
1. FRONTIER: Murray-Williams already prove NQP not in ACC0. A further fast SAT algorithm for ACC0 yields nothing new by itself; a result
   counts only if it opens an ADDITIONAL circuit class or a STRONGER lower bound (e.g. NP-level, or a class beyond ACC0).
2. GF(3) IS NOT "ONE MOD3 LAYER": a single mod-3 equation is one MOD3 gate, but solving an input-dependent linear system is a different
   task. FruitStack must show that the kernel parametrisation gives a PROVABLE worst-case saving for ALL circuits of an exactly defined
   class C: T_C(n, s) <= 2^n / n^omega(1) for every fixed polynomial size bound s(n), total time including all reductions and
   precomputation, and under the hypotheses of the concrete Williams-type transfer theorem being invoked (cite it, state its hypotheses).
3. Williams' method is one route among others (diagonalisation, circuit-complexity arguments in other models); the choice must be argued.
Until such a theorem exists, level 3 is OPEN. Finite tests are not a level-3 result.

## Level 3 — OPEN; next check (owner 2026-11-20): direct linear representation vs existential auxiliary variables
C0 (no auxiliary variables): Theorem A (saving min(2^{n/2}, 2^{n-rank})) and Theorem O (OR not representable) - archived, settled.
C0-exists: f(x) = exists y in {0,1}^m : A (x,y) = b (mod 3). Auxiliary variables must be typed: Boolean existential (this class),
uniquely determined by the system (functional), or GF(3)-valued (changes the Boolean problem - not allowed silently).
Questions: (1) which functions C0-exists represents (OR is representable with auxiliary variables - make this exact) and how m grows with
the function; (2) the saving measured in the ORIGINAL inputs n: Theorem A gives poly * min(2^{(n+m)/2}, 2^{n+m-rank}); it beats 2^n
only if m < n (first term) or n+m-rank < n, i.e. auxiliaries largely DETERMINED by the system - quantify the number of undetermined
auxiliaries needed per function / class; (3) whether a class between C0 and C0-exists (e.g. auxiliaries functionally determined by
few equations) is both closed enough for a Williams-type transfer and still solvable in 2^n/n^omega(1); (4) relation to the 3-colouring
family F(G), where the slacks are Boolean and determined (y = 1 - x_u - x_v) - why that keeps d = 2|V|.
Lemma F' (2026-11-20): if the auxiliary columns have full column rank k, time poly * 2^{n-(rank-k)} in the ORIGINAL n (pivots taken in
the auxiliaries); saving iff rank > k. The open question (3) is therefore: which functions/classes admit representations with
DETERMINED auxiliaries and rank > k, and is such a class closed enough for a transfer.
Status discipline: finite checks are evidence, not level-3 results; claims carry their exact quantified model.

## Level 3 next check, sharpened (2026-11-20): Boolean vs linear determinacy of auxiliaries
An auxiliary can be Boolean-determined (e.g. y = x1 AND x2 via a 1-in-3 gadget) without being determined by the GF(3) system; its column
then adds no rank and Lemma F' does not see it. Exploitation by RESTRICTION: choose S subset of x such that after fixing S every auxiliary
dependency is linear; then Lemma F' on the residual system gives total time 2^{n - (r_S - k_S)} * poly (|S| cancels), r_S = rank and
k_S = undetermined auxiliaries after the restriction. Saving = equation surplus r_S - k_S. Check on F(G): r - k = |V| -> 2^{2|V|}
(matches d = 2|V|). Open quantity: the guaranteed surplus r_S - k_S, under the best linearising restriction, for a circuit class closed
under the operations of a cited Williams-type transfer - is it omega(log n) (saving) or O(log n) for every such class (obstruction)?
Note (peer text, corrected): "rank > k" here is the GF(3) rank of the whole system vs the number of auxiliaries, NOT a target
function's polynomial/tensor rank.
