# PNP-001 priority direction — positive 1-in-3-SAT against circuits with mod-2 and mod-3 gates (owner decision 2026-11-19)

Binding. Research and verification only; no infrastructure changes. Goal: a provable statement about EVERY circuit of a class (not about a
proof system). Not a P != NP claim; the target ceiling is NP not in ACC0 (open; best known NQP not in ACC0, Williams / Murray-Williams).

## Why this direction
GF(3)-blind cores are exactly the unsatisfiable formulas where GF(3) linear reasoning (consistent) and GF(2) linear reasoning (x = all-ones
always solves the XOR system) both fail. Combining moduli 2 and 3 is where circuit lower bounds stop: AC0[p] (p prime) has lower bounds via
low-degree GF(p) approximation (Razborov-Smolensky); for modulus 6 the method fails.

## Object
SAT_N^{1in3}: input = indicator bits of the triples present on n variables (N = C(n,3)); output 1 iff the positive 1-in-3 formula is
satisfiable. Also restrictions/projections of it (families such as the 3-colouring translation T(G), blind cores, GF(3)-consistent inputs).

## Level 1 (bounded, aim: complete proof)
(a) TRANSFER AUDIT first (it worked for the PC degree): which known circuit lower bounds carry over to SAT_N^{1in3} by projections /
AC0 reductions? E.g. if MOD_q or MAJORITY is a projection of SAT_N^{1in3} (gadgets with auxiliary variables), AC0[p] lower bounds (p != q)
follow from Razborov-Smolensky; state exactly which functions embed and how.
(b) Depth-2 classes SYM o AND_k and MOD_6 o AND_k: Hastad-Goldmann - size s gives a (k+1)-party NOF protocol of cost O(k log s). Is a NOF-hard
function (generalised inner product, Babai-Nisan-Szegedy, k < log n) a projection of SAT_N^{1in3} with the right partition? Then size
lower bounds for these classes follow. Keep fan-in, party count and partition explicit.

## Level 2 (approximating polynomials over Z_6)
Degree of pairs (GF(2) polynomial, GF(3) polynomial) - via CRT a Z_6 polynomial - that represent / weakly represent / approximate
SAT_N^{1in3} or its restrictions; known reference points: Barrington-Beigel-Rudich (symmetric Z_6 representations of OR of degree
O(sqrt n)), Razborov-Smolensky, Green-type weak-degree results. Test objects: blind cores (complete tables n <= 10), satisfiable controls,
the T(G) family. Question: does the GF(2)-and-GF(3) failure of linear reasoning on blind cores force high Z_6 approximation degree?

## Level 3 next check, sharpened (2026-11-20): Boolean vs linear determinacy of auxiliaries
An auxiliary can be Boolean-determined (e.g. y = x1 AND x2 via a 1-in-3 gadget) without being determined by the GF(3) system; its column
then adds no rank and Lemma F' does not see it. Exploitation by RESTRICTION: choose S subset of x such that after fixing S every auxiliary
dependency is linear; then Lemma F' on the residual system gives total time 2^{n - (r_S - k_S)} * poly (|S| cancels), r_S = rank and
k_S = undetermined auxiliaries after the restriction. Saving = equation surplus r_S - k_S. Check on F(G): r - k = |V| -> 2^{2|V|}
(matches d = 2|V|). Open quantity: the guaranteed surplus r_S - k_S, under the best linearising restriction, for a circuit class closed
under the operations of a cited Williams-type transfer - is it omega(log n) (saving) or O(log n) for every such class (obstruction)?
Note (peer text, corrected): "rank > k" here is the GF(3) rank of the whole system vs the number of auxiliaries, NOT a target
function's polynomial/tensor rank.

## Level 3 — second route (owner 2026-11-20): non-enumerating evaluation; measure "matchgate excess"
Basis: T41 (research/PNP-001/theory/T41-...-2026-11-18.md): D5/D6 (read<=2 parity = det over F2; cost poly*2^{|H|}, H = variables with
>= 3 occurrences), MG-3 (no common T in GL2 makes EXACT1_3 and EQ_d, d>=3, matchgates; sharp at d=2; independent review 2026-11-20),
Theorem P (parity of positive 1-in-3 is parity-P-complete), R3 (read<=3 counting #P-complete), §14b (no automatic quotient -> Pfaffian).
Parallel to the GF(3) route: there the affine part is free and undetermined auxiliaries cost surplus (Theorems H, B); here the
matchgate part is summed by one Pfaffian (internal variables are NOT enumerated - this is what Theorem B excludes for GF(3)) and the
non-matchgate part must be branched.
Definition (to be used by every claim on this route). A representation = signature grid Omega (constraint and variable signatures,
planar embedding or explicit crossover gadgets), a holographic basis T (GL2, or GL_{2^l} for super-signatures/gadgets of block size l,
stated explicitly), and the transformed signatures f_v^T. Matchgate = planar matchgate signature (Cai-Gorenstein MGI, Thm 2.1/5.1).
Matchgate excess lambda(Omega, T) = sum over non-matchgate vertices v of log2 |supp f_v^T| (fixing the incident edges of these
vertices leaves a matchgate grid; cost poly * 2^lambda, worst case over ALL instances, branch sum as in Theorem Sigma).
Counting semantics: the Holant value counts complete edge assignments (pairs, as algorithm R); SAT = value > 0 only over characteristic
0 with nonnegative weights - evaluation mod p can cancel; projected counting needs unique witnesses.
Question for agents: for a composition-closed circuit class (closed under AND/OR/NOT and fan-out as required by a cited Williams-type
transfer), is there a representation scheme with lambda <= n - omega(log n) for every circuit of size poly(n) (n = original inputs,
NOT circuit size s)? Or prove lambda >= n - O(log n) for a precisely quantified scheme (e.g. one GL2 basis, gadgets of arity <= a).
Known pressure point: translating a circuit creates Theta(s) fan-out/equality nodes; by MG-3 these are non-matchgates in a common GL2
basis together with EXACT1_3. Controls: read-twice instances (lambda = 0, D5), F(G) colouring encoding, Equality, expander + K4,
Tseitin/parsimonious images of circuits (Theorem P) with growing s. Calibration: savings on positive 1-in-3 instances alone are
not a lower-bound route (exponential X3SAT algorithms exist in the literature - cite exactly before use).

### Second route - binding precision (owner 2026-11-20): lambda_eff, topology, K_{3,3} control
lambda (local signatures only) is REPLACED by lambda_eff(C): it is computed on an EXACT representation that a uniform algorithm
constructs in time poly(n, s) from every circuit C of the class: a PLANAR signature grid in which every crossing is an explicit
crossover vertex X(a,b,c,d) = [a=c][b=d], every gadget/planarisation step is included, and every vertex that is not a planar
matchgate after the stated basis contributes log2 |supp f_v^T|. A minimum over representations that are not efficiently
constructible does not count. Target: lambda_eff(C) <= n - omega(log n) for all C of the class; cost poly * 2^{lambda_eff}.
Verified facts (lab/acc0-level3/holo/k33_control.py): the crossover is NOT a planar matchgate in the standard basis (|supp| = 4,
cost 2 per crossing); K_{3,3} (edge variables, vertex clauses): n = 9, m = 6, read-twice, local excess 0, #SAT = 6 = #perfect
matchings, #SAT mod 2 = 0 = det B (T41 D5). For the stated representation (standard basis, one constructed crossing) the excess
is 2: lambda_eff(K_{3,3}) <= 2 is an UPPER bound for that representation; minimality over all admissible representations is NOT
proved (correction owner 2026-11-20).
Consequences: (1) "read-twice: lambda = 0" holds only for PLANAR read-twice instances (with the planar embedding computed, e.g. by a
linear-time planarity test). In general, for the standard basis, lambda_eff <= 2 * cr(D) for a drawing D that the algorithm itself
CONSTRUCTS in polynomial time (e.g. any straight-line drawing: O(m^2) crossings) - NOT 2 * cr(G): an optimal drawing need not be
efficiently constructible (computing cr(G) is NP-hard), and a non-constructive optimum does not satisfy the uniformity requirement
(same distinction as for Theorem B: existence of a favourable representation is not its efficient construction). Exact counting of
perfect matchings is #P-complete on general graphs, so crossings carry the hardness. (2)
Decision vs counting: read-twice SAT is polynomial on every graph by matching (Edmonds), without Pfaffians; the Pfaffian route is a
COUNTING route and counts complete assignments. (3) Counting modulo 2 (and any fixed p) can vanish on satisfiable instances
(K_{3,3}: 6 = 0 mod 2); SAT from a count needs characteristic 0, or a randomised/multi-prime argument stated explicitly.
Mandatory controls for every lambda_eff claim: K_{3,3}, Equality, F(G), expander + K4, Tseitin/parsimonious circuit images of growing s.

   (not constructive or not large), (d) why it does not algebrise. Without (a)-(d) the claim is classified as model-restricted.

   Aaronson-Wigderson definition; their use alone does not establish that a proof algebrizes. Natural proofs applies only to
   constructive and large properties under a pseudorandom-function assumption. The filter is a checklist, not an impossibility claim.

## INVARIANCE MATRIX — binding research instrument (owner decision 2026-11-20; replaces the first matrix draft)
Purpose: record which complexity measure survives which change, with which loss, in which direction - or which change breaks it.
Theorem 12 and all existing evidence are preserved unchanged; this section only classifies them.

Binding rules
R1. Every cell states six fields: (1) the exact measure and its domain; (2) the transformation class or proof-system extension;
    (3) the precise direction of the inequality; (4) the loss factor and its dependence on instance size and parameters; (5) the current
    proof, counterexample or open lemma, with evidence; (6) whether it implies anything about DECISION complexity.
R2. Every cell is PROVED, BROKEN or OPEN, with exact hypotheses. PROVED requires a paper proof that passed independent review, or a
    published theorem checked at the source; the fast-validator state is recorded but does not decide (fast-lane limits are known).
    BROKEN requires a proved breaking construction. Finite experiments only support conjectures about a cell; they never fill it.
R3. Two kinds of rows, never mixed: [REP] representation change (the instance changes; the measure is compared across encodings) and
    [EXT] proof-system extension (the instance is fixed; the system becomes stronger).
R4. No decision-complexity conclusion follows from this matrix alone. In particular, invariance of a measure under polynomial-time
    transformations does NOT imply P != NP: that requires a separate, rigorous transfer from the measure to the running time of
    UNRESTRICTED decision algorithms, which none of these cells provides. Field (6) is "none" unless such a transfer is proved.
R5. Independent-validation requirements of this specification stay in force (independent review before PROVED; barrier filter for any
    claim about general algorithms or circuits).
