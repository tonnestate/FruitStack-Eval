## title

Separate projection of shared-auxiliary constraints does not commute with intersection

## mathematical_statement

Let X = {0,1}, Z = {0,1}, E1 = {(x,z) : x = z} and E2 = {(x,z) : x = 1 - z}. Then proj_X(E1) = proj_X(E2) = X, but proj_X(E1 intersect E2) is empty. Replacing constraints on shared variables Z by their separate projections can turn an inconsistent conjunction into a satisfiable one.

More generally, replacing constraints on shared variables Z by their separate projections can turn an inconsistent conjunction into a satisfiable one. Proof: For each x∈X, E1 has the unique witness z=x, and E2 has the unique witness z=1−x, so both projections equal X. A common witness would require z=x and z=1−x, impossible for either Boolean x; hence E1∩E2=∅ and its projection is empty.
