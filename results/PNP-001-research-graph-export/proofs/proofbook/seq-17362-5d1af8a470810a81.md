## Statement / proof text (verbatim)

IPS-SEARCH + CARRY-DICKSON (owner 2026-11-26/27, director check; CLAIMED). (0) Addendum to seq 17359: it omitted THEOREM 14 (seq 5169, full roABP-IPS with bounded placeholder degree), Lemma 15 (seq 5174, placeholder-monomial/width trade-off) and ALG-BIT-001 (seq 3686, 3687, 3727: XOR/carry encoding, degree-2 NS/PC certificates, finite); all are restricted-proof-system or finite results, so the strength map is unchanged. (1) VERIFIED BY AUTHOR: P = NP => PH = P => BPP = P (Sipser-Gacs-Lautemann) => PIT in P => IPS certificates are verifiable in P => the prefix predicate is in P and short IPS refutations are found bit by bit (length by doubling): P = NP => IPS proof search is automatable. This is seq 17136 (E-AUTOMATE, resolution) transferred to IPS. Only one direction is available for IPS ('automatable => P = NP' is Atserias-Mueller for resolution, not known for IPS), so 'IPS is not automatable' is a statement whose truth implies P != NP. 17136(b) applies verbatim: invariants of the certificates themselves (existence, size, annihilators, dual certificates, degree) are unconditional statements that also hold if P = NP, so they cannot bound findability against all algorithms; reductions of the Atserias-Mueller type give the converse direction (automatable => P = NP). BARRIER CHECK (promised for owner step 2): the transfer relativises (and algebrises), so a proof of non-automatability needs a non-relativising and non-algebrising step; no candidate step is named. MECHANISM EXCLUDED (extends 17136): invariant-based limits on IPS proof search as a route to P != NP. (2) CARRY-DICKSON, VERIFIED BY AUTHOR: XOR part solved by Gaussian elimination (x = x0 + By), carry equations become quadratics q_j; reducing modulo the span of their quadratic parts leaves r quadratic plus affine equations; Z = 2^{-r} sum_lambda sum_y (-1)^{lambda.q(y)}, each inner sum 0 or +-2^{e-k_lambda} by Dickson, computable in poly time; hence exact counting/decision in 2^r poly(|F|). Classical (Dickson/Arf; counting solutions of r quadratic equations over F_2 in 2^r poly time); the Dickson character sums were already used in the book (seq 846, 3973, 4020). It is an algorithm, i.e. an upper bound; for an NP-complete family r cannot be O(log n) on all instances unless P = NP, which is not provable here. Not a gap closure.
