## Statement / proof text (verbatim)

Q-BD (T43 sec.5) symbolically: linearization theorem checked, and its limit proved. CLAIMED, Claude family; literature marked.
(L1, owner's theorem, checked) Use GF(2) gate algebra: NOT=1+x, XOR=x+y, AND=xy. Process the gates in topological order and keep every gate as an affine form on the current affine piece P. If an AND input a is non-constant on P, split P into {a=0} and {a=1}; this lowers dim P by 1, and the gate becomes the affine form 0 or b on the two parts. If a is constant on P, no split is needed. The pieces form a parity decision tree of depth <= min(n,m), so there are <= 2^min(n,m) disjoint leaves. On each leaf the output is affine, and SAT on a leaf is linear algebra. Total time O*(2^min(n,m)). This is folklore-type and its novelty is not claimed.
(L2, extension) A leaf may stop early when every remaining gate has degree <= 2 on it. Then SAT of a quadratic GF(2) form is decided in poly time by Dickson's theorem (from memory; classical). Call A2 the class of algorithms that cover F2^n with affine pieces, by any procedure, and keep every gate of C as an explicit polynomial of degree <= 2 on each piece. A2 contains L1, the hybrid/merging calculus of 17917/17919 in its gate-wise form, and the AND-to-linear rule.
(L3, barrier theorem, proof) Suppose D: F2^n -> F2 is non-constant on every affine subspace of dimension d (an affine disperser). Then (i) D is not affine on any affine subspace V with dim V >= d+1. Proof: if D|V = l + c with l linear, then D is constant on the coset V cap {l=const}, whose dimension is >= d. (ii) D does not have degree <= 2 on any affine subspace V with dim V >= 2d+2. Proof: by Dickson normal form, a quadratic form on V of dimension k is affine on an affine subspace of dimension >= ceil(k/2) (fix one variable of each hyperbolic pair); then apply (i). Hence every A2 cover of a circuit containing a gate that computes D has >= 2^(n-2d-2) pieces. With C = D AND NOT D (unsatisfiable), no early exit through a satisfying leaf is possible. Consequence: if there is a poly-time computable D with d = O(log n), then every A2 algorithm needs time >= 2^n/poly(n) on some poly-size circuits. The n^{omega(1)} saving required by Williams/Q-BD is then impossible inside A2.
(Literature, from memory and search, to verify at the source) Li, ECCC TR23-023 (FOCS 2023): explicit affine extractors for entropy O(log n) with constant error. If that error is < 1/2, this gives the needed disperser with d = O(log n). Li 2015 (entropy polylog n) is not enough: it only gives the bound 2^(n - polylog n), which does not exclude a superpolynomial saving. That parity decision trees of affine dispersers have depth >= n - d is likely known (folklore). L3 is therefore expected to be known in substance; novelty is not claimed.
(Transfer to phi_C) A 1-in-3 clause is x+y+z=1 (affine) together with xy=yz=xz=0 (quadratic). The affine part therefore costs nothing (Gauss/hybrid). All the hardness lies in the quadratic constraints, so it is the same class A2 again. The exact transfer of L3 to Lemma-B images is not proved separately.
(Status) Not a lower-bound premise. It is an unconditional statement about an algorithm class: given the construction from the literature, it is a theorem. Linearization, Dickson leaves and gate-wise merging cannot by themselves give the Q-BD saving. Any Q-BD saving must contain a step outside A2. There are only two options: (a) the pieces are not affine, so they are varieties or sumsets; or (b) pieces are certified to be zero without making the gates explicit on them, which is global cancellation such as D AND NOT D = 0. Option (b) is the merging/normal-form problem of 17917 (Lemma G) and 17925, so it is not cheaper. Even a solved Q-BD gives only NEXP not in P/poly, not P != NP, and holds under P=NP as well (owner).
