ELIMINATION WITH MERGING: RULE INCOMPLETENESS AND THE GAUSS CONTROL (Fable addendum A3/A6 tested; CLAIMED, Claude family). (1) Incompleteness: x=0, x=1, y=0, y=1 and the point (2,2) cover F3^2. prune+merge (scratchpad/aelim/merge.py) stops at 5 subspaces, where 1 suffices. The decision itself is correct (decide_m = covered). Rule set recorded as incomplete. The restriction-merge rule (resolution on a linear form) is not added, because a complete procedure decides 3-colourability (Fable Lemma G), so completeness is the decision problem itself. (2) Control (a), dense random linear systems over F3 with k equations in k variables, bad set = 2 hyperplanes per equation (Gauss-solvable): peak subspace count 28 at k=6, 278 at k=8; k=10 did not finish in 300 s. Brute force agrees. So the procedure blows up on instances that Gaussian elimination solves in polynomial time. Its subspace count measures this representation, not the difficulty of the instance. Consistent with Fable Lemma K (cube {0,1}^k, acn = 2^k, from a treewidth-2 instance). Controls (b) and (c) not run: (a) already settles the interpretation. Consequence for the contract (par. 6): counts from 17910/17911 and later G versus Prism(G) comparisons carry no hardness reading. Under fixed orders they compare only representations. The exact open step of this line (Fable A4) is a dag-like lower bound for resolution over linear equations mod 3. That is an open problem in proof complexity, would bound one algorithm family only, and is not an obligation of PNP-001. No obligation closed.
