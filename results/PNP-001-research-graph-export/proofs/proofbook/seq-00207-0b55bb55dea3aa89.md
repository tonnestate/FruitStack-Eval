## Statement / proof text (verbatim)

REJECTED: the proposed extension of the null-cone lemma to NON-rectangular partitions, and the claimed d_min^sep(3,4) >= 9. MATH ERROR: for a non-rectangular lambda with ell=k, the HWV transforms as P_lambda(g(t).f) = t^w P_lambda(f) with w = k*lambda_1 - |lambda| != 0. Then lim_{t->0} t^w P_lambda(f) = 0 for w>0 holds for ANY value of P_lambda(f), giving NO information - one CANNOT conclude P_lambda(f)=0. The null-cone argument works ONLY for w=0 (rectangular lambda=(d^k) = SL_k-invariant), where P_lambda(g(t).f)=P_lambda(f) is constant so P_lambda(f)=P_lambda(0)=0. ADVERSARIAL CONFIRMATION (exact computation): lambda=(12,2,2,2,2) has w=5*12-20=40>0; the flawed lemma predicts vanishing on Y, but the HWV evaluates NONZERO on z*perm_3 (e.g. 103363705/13824, 1785096875/27 at random 5-planes) => delta_perm=1, consistent with my original d=5 report (22 of 23 five-row HWVs non-vanishing on perm). Direct contradiction => extension invalid. CORRECTED STATUS: only d_min^sep(3,4) >= 6 stands (from rectangular T11-N + BHIM symmetroid bound for ell<=4 up to degree 8 + the 23 five-row d=5 certificates). d_min^sep(3,4) >= 9 is NOT proven. LMR PRECISIONS (owner): LMR Thm 3.1.1 gives degree-2n(n-1) equations but does NOT prove this is the MINIMAL separation degree; LMR equations are not automatically certified separators without checking non-vanishing on Y; the Hessian criterion concerns rank at points of the hypersurface {f=0}, not the whole space (Hess(det_n) at the identity is non-degenerate). So 'degree 24 = proven minimum' is unjustified. VALID OPEN TASK: for d=6,7,8 and non-rectangular ell>=5 partitions, determine structurally whether ker E_D not subset ker E_Y can occur; no valid null-cone shortcut exists for the non-rectangular case.
