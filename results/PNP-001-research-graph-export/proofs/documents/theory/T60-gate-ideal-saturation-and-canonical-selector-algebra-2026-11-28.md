## 1. Gate-ideal saturation theorem [P]
Ring R=F_p[t,v_1..v_N]/(v_i^p−t^{p−1}v_i) (p=2: v²=tv), free over F_p[t] (T54 1.1), t a non-zerodivisor; ρ:R→B[t], v↦tv injective, B=F_p[v]/(v^p−v).
F2: gate residual r_j∈{0,1} (violation indicator G_j:=r_j, e.g. y+ab), H_j its degree-2 homogenization (AND: yt−ab; NOT: t(y+t+a)); ρ(H_j)=t²G_j. J_C=(H_1..H_s), P_C=∏_j(t²−H_j), ρ(P_C)=t^{2s}∏V_j, V_j=1+G_j (validity).
Theorem. (i) H_jP_C=0. (ii) t^{2s}−P_C=Σ_j H_j·t^{2(j−1)}∏_{i>j}(t²−H_i)∈J_C. (iii) J_C:t^∞=J_C:t^{2s}=Ann_R(P_C)=ρ^{-1}((G_1..G_s)B[t]) = homogeneous Rees ideal of the valid-computation graph.
Proof. (i) ρ(H_jP_C)=t^{2s+2}G_j∏V_i=0 because G_jV_j=G_j+G_j²=0 (G_j idempotent); ρ injective. (ii) telescoping T_j=t^{2j}∏_{i>j}(t²−H_i): T_j−T_{j−1}=t^{2(j−1)}H_j∏_{i>j}(t²−H_i), T_s=t^{2s}, T_0=P_C. (iii) J_C⊆Ann(P_C) by (i). f∈Ann(P_C) ⇒ t^{2s}f=(t^{2s}−P_C)f∈J_C by (ii): Ann⊆J_C:t^{2s}. If t^kf∈J_C then t^kfP_C=0 ⇒ fP_C=0 (t non-zerodivisor): J_C:t^∞⊆Ann(P_C)⊆J_C:t^{2s}⊆J_C:t^∞. Last equality: fP_C=0 ⇔ f·∏V=0 in B ⇔ ρ(f) vanishes on the valid set ⇔ ρ(f)∈(G_j)B[t] (B is a ring of functions, the ideal of the common zero set of the idempotents G_j is (G_j)). ∎
Machine check (F2, 3-gate circuit y1=a∧b, y2=¬y1, y3=y2∧a): (i),(ii) hold; for f=y1y2 (vanishes on valid graph) the least k with t^k f∈J_C is 1 ≤ 2s=6; for f=y3t+a y2 it is 0. The exponent 2s is an UPPER bound, not tight.
Limits: bounds the t-power only. It gives explicit cofactors of poly-size CIRCUIT (O(s) ring operations) for the gate part, but "P_C·f=0 in R" is itself a Boolean-reduction identity of a degree 2s+deg f polynomial; its certificate is uncontrolled (coNP-hard to check, T48 3b). So no poly-size IPS refutation follows.

## 2. F3 and general p [P; machine check on 2 ternary Boolean-restricted variables]
Let gate residual r_j take values in S_j⊆F_p, k_j=|S_j∖{0}|≤p−1, H_j degree d_j. P_j:=∏_{c∈S_j∖0}(t^{d_j}−c^{-1}H_j) has ρ(P_j)=t^{d_jk_j}V_j, V_j=∏_c(1−r_j/c)=[r_j=0]. Then H_jP_j=0 (rV=0), t^{d_jk_j}−P_j∈(H_j). Hence the theorem holds with exponent
  D = Σ_j d_j k_j ≤ (p−1)Σ_j d_j,  J_C:t^∞=J_C:t^D=Ann(P_C), P_C=∏P_j.
(Generic validity indicator 1−r^{p−1} is k_j=p−1.) F2: k_j=1, D=Σd_j=2s for quadrics. Boolean-coordinate restriction vs ternary: if variables are ternary (v³=t²v) and Boolean-ness is imposed by generators H'_v=v²−tv (d=2), they count in D (k: residual v²−v takes values {0,−1}, so k=1 and a LINEAR factor suffices: with q_v=v−2t, t²−q_v²=−H'_v in R_3 and H'_vq_v=0 — checked). Hence Q^h=∏(y_i−2t) of T52/T54 is a square root of the validity factor ∏(t²+H'_i): T52's functional QE factor is the half-degree form of the same annihilator construction. If instead all variables are Boolean-restricted (v²=tv over F3) the Rees ring is the p=2-type ring and Boolean generators are already relations of R.

## 3. Combination with T49/T54/T55 [P unless marked]
Combined circuit C: gates for W(G), R(G,x), R(G,W(G)), outputs o_1,o_2; Φ^g:=o_1(t−o_2), ρ(Φ^g)=t²o_1(1+o_2) (F2).
(a) (*) ⇔ P_CΦ^g=0 ⇔ Φ^g∈J_C:t^{2s} ⇔ t^{2s}Φ^g∈J_C. Exact restatement; with explicit part t^{2s}Φ^g=Σ_jH_jc_j+P_CΦ^g.
(b) Elimination: for E=J_C∩R_{G,x} (gate variables eliminated) E:t^∞=E:t^{2s}. Proof: f∈R_{G,x}, t^kf∈E ⇒ f∈J_C:t^∞=J_C:t^{2s} ⇒ t^{2s}f∈J_C∩R_{G,x}. So the saturation exponent SURVIVES elimination.
(c) Decoder: with gate variables retained, L̃:R_{n+m}→R_{n+s}/J_C^{sat}, z_j↦w_j (output gate variable) is a degree-preserving (unit weight) algebra hom (w_j³−t²w_j=0 holds; it is a generator) realizing L_S on the cube; the unit-weight obstruction of R3 disappears at the price of s extra variables and of the ideal J_C^{sat}=J_C:t^{2s}. After elimination the decoder needs the weight shift: in R/J^{sat}, t^{d_j−1}w_j=S̄_j^h (d_j=deg S̄_j) — this is exactly the weighted continuation of T59 §8. Eliminating gate variables costs t^{deg S̄_j −1}, a quantity up to N and unrelated to s.
(d) What does NOT survive elimination without further assumption: small generating sets of E, the valuation (G) of explicit substituted polynomials (squaring chain), formal size of the reduced multilinear graph indicator (§5), formal QE over internal variables (T53: VNP-complete over F3, nonuniform). Canonical selection adds only: uniform poly s (P=NP) and uniqueness of the valid graph (functional, so ρ-valid set is the graph of a function).
(e) Φ_W under P=NP: correctness is a statement about the poly-size circuit P_CΦ^g in R, equivalent to the coNP-type identity; the saturation exponent is polynomial but this does not make the identity easier. [no new contradiction]

## 4. Hrubeš transfer [CL, user-derived; checked mechanics only]
Affine Boolean selectors ℓ_j over F2 (parities), R(x,z)=[z=ℓ(x)]: functional bundle, N(x)=1, selector poly-time, affine (so the weighted/unit Rees continuation exists), trivial fibre symmetry. Λ=∏(1+z_j+ℓ_j(x)). Checked: restriction z:=1 commutes with ML (ML is reduction mod I; substitution of constants is a ring hom) and does not increase formal size; in characteristic 2, 1+1=0, so ML(Λ)|_{z=1}=ML(∏_jℓ_j(x)). Hence if ML of products of affine forms in ≤3 variables is VNP-hard (Hrubeš Thm 7 as reported by the user; I have not read the theorem statement, field/characteristic hypotheses unverified), then the reduced graph indicator of an affine functional bundle can be VNP-hard even though selector, gate ideal (s=O(mn) gates, saturation ≤2s) and Rees continuation are all small. This SEPARATES gate-ideal saturation from multilinearization: both statements can hold at once. It refutes the inference "unique witness / affine selector / trivial fibre symmetry / graded continuation / efficient functional substitution ⇒ efficient reduced formal graph polynomial" (conditional on VP≠VNP). No contradiction from P=NP.

## 7. Affine graph ideal has saturation exponent 0 (user, verified 2026-11-28)
For affine S_j=a_{j0}+Σa_{ji}x_i over F2 (Hrubeš Thm 7 system z_01=x_1+1, z_10=x_2+1, z_11=x_1+x_2+1 is of this type; the original text was not read by me): (S_j^h)²=tS_j^h in R_{2,n} (char 2: cross terms vanish; machine-checked for all 8 affine forms in 2 variables), so z_j↦S_j^h is a well-defined graded hom and R_{2,n+m}/(z_j−S_j^h)≅R_{2,n}. t is a non-zerodivisor on R_{2,n}, hence J:t^∞=J (exponent 0). This is the unit-weight affine case of T59 R3, not a new mechanism. With §4 (Hrubeš, unconditional VNP-hardness of ML; superpolynomial formal size needs VP≠VNP) it shows: even exponent 0, unique witness, affine selector, graded decoder do not give an efficient reduced formal graph polynomial. Saturation is not the algorithmic difficulty of witness selection. No contradiction from P=NP.
