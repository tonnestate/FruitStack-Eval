## 1. What P = NP forces (exact)
Let R(G,x) be the verifier (3COL / 1-in-3). P=NP ⇒ self-reducibility gives uniform poly-time W, W(G)=lexicographically least witness if G∈L, W(G)=any fixed string (e.g. 0^n) otherwise; note the recursion of §7/R2 would output 1^m on G∉L — a convention, irrelevant since R(G,·)=0 there. Totality needs no extra condition.
- (C1) [PN] Skolem form (T49 L2): A(G)=R(G,W(G)) decides L; correctness ⇔ (*) R(G,x)(1−R(G,W(G)))=0 on Boolean (G,x). Equivalent to P=NP (uniform W) / NP⊆P/poly (circuits). Not a solution.
- (C2) [PN] Canonical uniquification R'(G,x)=R(G,x)∧[x=W(G)] ∈ P: exactly one witness per G∈L; ∨_x R = Σ_x R' over ℤ (T53 F1). A unique witness gives a unique extension of the *encoded* gadget system only if the encoding is parsimonious (T41 §6); it does NOT give a unique accepting computation path of a given machine, nor a small formal counting polynomial (needs MT).
- (C3) [PN] PH=P, E∈P, NP⊆SPP (T53 F2); G_E ∈ VNP_p for all p (F3) — [U] equivalent to NP⊆Mod_pP/poly.
- (C4) [PN] T_S∘L_R=M_E with S=W a poly-size operator. Definitions (T55): L_R: a↦aR (multiplication by R on functions of (G,x)); T_S: g↦g(·,S(·)); then T_S L_R a=a·R(·,S(·)); for a correct S this equals a·E, i.e. M_E (multiplication by χ_L). ker L_R=(1−E)·B. [U] A correct S always exists (exponential size); P=NP only makes T_S realizable by a poly-size circuit. So every property of T_S true for all correct S is [U] and cannot contradict.
Not forced (do not assume): FP=#P, P=⊕P, VP=VNP, efficient formal elimination. [NEW verified] Canonical selection does NOT turn the formal polynomial of the graph of W into a VP polynomial: it is the multilinear extension of [U=W(G)], in VNP, and its VP-membership is MT (T50).

## 7. Does canonical uniform selection force an algebraic realization of T_S? (2026-11-28)
Answer: only a restatement of (*); compatibility with saturation and gate algebra is automatic and constrains nothing. Details:
- (R1) [U] Realization as a map is forced for EVERY correct S: T_S is the substitution homomorphism z_j ↦ S_j in B=F_p[X]/I (Gelfand/Stone, T55). Not specific to P=NP.
- (R2) [PN, lexmin] Triangular telescoping. S_j(x)=1−E_j(x,S_{<j}(x)), E_j(x,u)=∃z_{>j} R(x,u,0,z_{>j}) (an NP predicate, in P under P=NP). So T_S is a product of m one-variable existential eliminations; one-variable elimination in B is OR(a,b)=a+b−ab (a gate of T48). Λ(x,z)=[z=S(x)]=Π(1+z_i+S_i(x)) (T56 §2). The product form is formal: each ∃ over z_{>j} is an exponential OR and a step may double circuit size; poly size of the whole family IS the content of the claim. Uniform poly-size triangular family ⇔ P=NP; non-uniform ⇔ NP⊆P/poly. Not a new consequence.
- (R3) [NEW, saturation] A degree-preserving graded realization on the Rees algebra R=F_p[t,X]/J requires ρ(z_j)=t·S̄_j with t·S̄_j ∈ ρ(R_1)=t·(affine forms), i.e. every S_j affine in x (Boolean-valued: literals/constants over F3; parities allowed over F2). For non-affine S_j one must give z_j the weight deg S̄_j (weighted Rees algebra, J_w=(z_j^p − t^{(p−1)w_j} z_j)); the substitution then has t-valuation Σ_gates (formal degree − reduced degree) by Lemma 1.2 (g^h = t^{deg g − deg f̄} f̄^h). This valuation is exactly the Boolean (ideal) cancellation of T48; P=NP gives no bound on it that I can prove (the trivial per-gate bound is N; 's·N' is heuristic, not proved). The affine case matches T52 (witnesses y_i=a_i(x)+ℓ_i(z) are affine).
- (R4) Compatibility: all identities are identities in B; R=Rees(B,F) with I^h=J (T54 1.1/1.2), so every identity transfers to the graded setting. Nothing is violated; nothing is forced beyond the weights in R3.
Conclusion: the realization forced by P=NP is "triangular poly-size composition of OR/AND/NOT substitutions in B, graded with weights = reduced degrees"; its only free quantity is the saturation valuation along gates = E.4 for Boolean circuits (T54/T55), the first unproved implication of §5. ## 8. Weighted Rees continuation (owner/Grok statement, checked 2026-11-28)
Claim: z_j ↦ t^{d_j−deg S̄_j}·S̄_j^h with weights deg x_i=1, deg z_j=d_j ≥ deg S̄_j and relations z_j^p=t^{d_j(p−1)}z_j is a graded homomorphism R^w_{p,n+m}→R_{p,n} fixing x. Check: ρ(S̄^h)=t^{deg S̄}S̄, so ρ(image)=t^{d_j}S_j; (t^{d}S)^p=t^{dp}S^p=t^{dp}S (S F_p-valued) = t^{d(p−1)}·t^{d}S ✓. Leading terms z_j^p stay coprime (Gröbner, freeness over F_p[t] as in T54 1.1). Exactness criterion: with unit weights (d_j=1) a continuation exists iff deg S̄_j ≤ 1 (=R3). Consistent with R3; the explicit formula is the same as R3's weighted Rees algebra. Facts: (i) it exists for EVERY selector, efficient or not, so no contradiction from existence; (ii) weights d_j=deg S̄_j are invariants of the function S_j (reduced F_p-degree ≤ N), the saturation defect δ(g)=formal degree − reduced degree per gate (Lemma 1.2) is an invariant of the circuit; for products (ab)^h=a^h b^h=t^{deg a+deg b−deg\bar{ab}}·\bar{ab}^h, so defects add along a circuit: total valuation = Σ_gates δ_g (additivity, unconditional). ## 10. Selector elimination Q_S vs. the algebraic cost of a Boolean sum (2026-11-28)
Literature (checked at abstract level only, 2026-11-28): Orzel–Srinivasan–Tavenas–Yehudayoff, "The Algebraic Cost of a Boolean Sum", arXiv 2502.02442 / ECCC TR25-099 / FSTTCS 2025: α_f(s)=min k such that a Boolean sum over s variables is a simple projection of f_{n+k}; permanent has bounded cost (2-variable sum simulated at additive constant), determinant does not (cost linear/∞; also in the border setting). The exact numbers 'α_perm(2)≤3, α_det(2)=∞' and Thm numbering were NOT verified. [EXT] Krentel 1988: lexicographically maximum satisfying assignment is FP^NP-complete (from memory, unverified) — consistent with C2: canonical selection ∈ FP ⇔ P=NP.
[NEW, proved] Q_S as substitution. Let Λ_S(x,z)=Π_i((1−S_i)(1−z_i)+S_i z_i) with S_i polynomials. For g multilinear in z (any characteristic): Q_S(g):=Σ_{z∈{0,1}^m} Λ_S g = g(x,S(x)) IDENTICALLY in F[X] (induct on z_i: Σ_{z_i∈{0,1}}((1−S_i)(1−z_i)+S_i z_i)g = (1−S_i)g|_{0}+S_i g|_{1}=g|_{z_i=S_i}). So selector elimination is not a Boolean sum with new cost; it is composition g∘(x,S̄).
Consequences: (1) If every S̄_i is affine (the R3/§8 case), Q_S is a projection: cost 0 in any projection-closed family (perm, det). (2) Otherwise the cost is the size of S̄_i inside the family F; taking g=z_1 gives Q_S(z_1)=S̄_1, so closure of F under Q_S REQUIRES S̄_1∈F; for VP (closed under composition) it is also sufficient. (3) Under P=NP, S̄_i is the multilinear extension of a P-computable function: ∈VNP (Valiant), ∈VP iff the MT-type statement for that function (Hrubeš TR15-067: false in general for P functions). So closure of VP under Q_S for the canonical selector ⇔ S̄_i ∈ VP — the same first unproved implication (§5(a)); selector elimination does not move it. (4) The α-cost theorem separates perm/det for UNRESTRICTED Boolean sums (E≡1), which is the VP-vs-VNP direction; it says nothing about Q_S, because Q_S has a single term and no sum. Not a new route to a contradiction; at most a way to restate: det family closed under Q_S for the canonical S ⇔ determinantal complexity of S̄_i polynomial.
