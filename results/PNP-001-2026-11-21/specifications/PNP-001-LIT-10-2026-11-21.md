## Atserias-Mueller, "Simple general magnification of circuit lower bounds", arXiv:2503.24061 (v1 2025-03-31, v2 2025-06-21)
Verified from the arXiv abstract page:
- Fixed-polynomial formula-size lower bounds for NP are implied by slightly superlinear formula-size lower bounds for APPROXIMATING any
  sufficiently sparse problem in NP.
- Central tool: "distinguishers" - sparse matrices that keep key properties of error-correcting codes.
- They generalize and strengthen the general (not problem-specific) magnification results of Chen-Jin-Williams 2020 and reach
  magnification thresholds below known lower bounds.
- A uniform magnification result for MCSP that "seems to sidestep" the localization barrier (authors' wording).
NOT verified here: exact thresholds, the uniform separation obtained, the transfer target. Read the paper before citing a theorem.
Note: the formula consequence is "NP not in fixed-polynomial FORMULA size" - not P != NP.

## Chukhin-Gajulapalli-Golovnev-Mihajlin, "A Little Derandomization Goes a Long Way: Unconditional Circuit Lower Bounds using
## Low-Degree PIT", arXiv:2611.07972 (submitted 2026-11-05, cs.CC)
Verified from the arXiv abstract page:
- Unconditional: either NE contains a language requiring Boolean circuits of size at least 2^n/(2n), or VP^0_Z != VNP^0_Z (a
  constant-free version of Valiant's conjecture over the integers). The Boolean alternative implies NP not in SIZE[n^k] for every k.
- A deterministic O(s t^{d-1})-time PIT algorithm for size-s circuits computing degree-d polynomials in t variables over a fixed
  finite field F.
- Deterministic PIT for syntactically set-multilinear circuits in time s^{O(1)} t^{5d/6}; a range-avoidance-based arithmetic dichotomy
  from an NP algorithm for PIT over Z.
NOT verified: the statement that a (nondeterministic) PIT algorithm in time O(s t^{d-1-eps}) implies new lower bounds ("Theorem 3" in an
owner-supplied summary) - read the paper body (arxiv.org/html/2611.07972v1) and quote the exact theorem before building on it.

## Algebraic annihilators for range avoidance (owner-supplied construction, checked by the director)
- If P != 0 over GF(2) with P(G(x)) = 0 for all x, every y with P(y) = 1 is outside Im(G). Example G(x) = (x1x2, x1x3, x2x3, x1x2x3):
  y1*y2 = y4 on the image, so (1,1,0,0) is not in the image - correct.
- Known method (annihilators; complexity studied in the literature - find and cite the exact reference before claiming anything).
- Caveats: for the circuit-evaluation generator (circuit description -> truth table) low-degree annihilators need not exist; a dense,
  efficiently testable algebraic property of truth tables risks the natural-proofs barrier; a hard function constructed in time
  poly(truth table) gives only exponential-time hardness without a further transfer.

## Rank-one THR o THR speed-up (owner-supplied, checked by the director)
- If k variables have proportional weight columns W_ij = a_i v_j (j in J), all bottom gates see x_J only through z = sum_j v_j x_j;
  enumerating the other n-k variables and counting z in the O(m) relevant intervals by meet-in-the-middle gives 2^{n-k/2} poly(n,m,L).
  Correct, but a strong structural restriction. Natural generalisation for lane B / C: weight matrices of low rank r (bottom gates
  depend on r linear forms), and approximate versions - with polynomially bounded weights the number of distinct (Wx) vectors is small.
